# A1-A2 curriculum working draft

Updated: 2026-10-22. Status: outline and brief familiar-foundations approach accepted by the owner on 2026-10-08; detailed lessons remain a working draft, not an approved final syllabus, authored course, or implementation. Confirmed scope and product decisions remain in [COURSE-DESIGN.md](COURSE-DESIGN.md), especially C-024 through C-029. This document owns the detailed A1-A2 curriculum work.

## Purpose and boundaries

Build from beginner foundations to a strong A2-aligned reading/writing/listening curriculum, with grammar and vocabulary supporting practical written tasks. Repeated practice and personal phrases operate throughout. Speaking/conversation is excluded; listening targeting Spain Spanish with adaptive auditory-recognition practice is included under C-035/C-036; C-040 permits reviewed synthetic speech as the initial audio route. Short everyday-phrase audio exercises are included in the first usable stage under C-037; detailed coverage, sources, and objective mapping remain pending. No overall CEFR proficiency claim is implied.

Spanish of Spain is the main variety (C-028). The initial interface, instructions, explanations, hints, and feedback are Ukrainian only, with future localization planned under C-030/C-031. Spanish remains the language being studied.

The map below is an original teaching sequence accepted as the planning outline under C-029, informed by sources S1-S3. It is not the sequence prescribed by Instituto Cervantes, and the grouping is not a fixed lesson count. Each row will need smaller lessons, reviewed examples, explicit accepted answers, and delayed checks. Topic familiarity may change pacing; it must not fabricate mastery evidence.

## Threads that must not disappear between blocks

- Situations: identity, relationships, home, daily routine, food, shopping, transport, time, study/work, leisure, appointments, and simple problems. These are proposed context families, not a fixed word list.
- Reading and writing: introduce short notices, forms, descriptions, schedules, and messages throughout; do not postpone all meaningful text work until the final block.
- Language detail: expand questions/negation, pronouns, prepositions, quantifiers, comparatives, connectors, and orthography alongside the headline verb topics. A tense-only syllabus would not meet the proposed curriculum.
- Learning evidence: distinguish recognizing, recalling a form, using it in a sentence, and selecting it in context. Track targeted hints and answer reveals locally to the assessed skill.
- Personal phrases: allow saving from a lesson, adding an external phrase, and capturing Ukrainian intent; only checked entries become practice models. Reuse both phrases and appropriate variations.
- Listening: build from everyday phrases to sentences and brief connected texts, targeting Spain pronunciation with reviewed synthetic speech initially and suitable existing human recordings as later additions (C-040). Map listening targets and recording variants alongside the written curriculum; keep recognition, meaning, transcript-assisted work, and written production distinct. Initial short-phrase listening inclusion is settled under C-037; detailed module allocation remains open.
- Review: revisit old skills in new but familiar contexts, including later sessions; initial intervals are accepted under C-057, while the detailed queue/override algorithm remains open. Do not introduce unknown grammar into a supposed recall check without explaining it.

Lesson text, word lists, tasks, and audio remain unprepared/unreviewed; this is not a published lesson corpus. Practical outcome: recognize and enter the written forms needed by later short words, labels, and questions, preserving relevant accents and punctuation. - Apply C-061's approximate 8-12-task starter target where suitable to a small writing objective, calibrated internally; do not demand that count for every character or every saved word. Include recognition, entry/correction, and distinct later prompts as appropriate.

- Start with a concise Ukrainian reminder; offer a more targeted clue, a reviewed model, or answer reveal on request. Interface-only shortcut help is not language assistance. Preserve actual assistance in the relevant evidence.
- Use short familiar contexts such as a label, name, or greeting. Only reviewed source items can be saved as ready personal phrases; a learner-created expression follows the draft/review path.
- Check actual text entry and help with Chrome on macOS and the planned iPhone test browser. These are pending checks, not validated keyboard instructions.
- Full word lists, exact answer variants, audio allocation, linguistic review, and graded checkpoint items are still to be authored/reviewed. Additional orthography coverage must be mapped elsewhere; this block is not a claim of complete inventory coverage.

## Accepted block design: A1-02 — naming and describing

Status: grouping/direction accepted on 2026-10-22 under C-064 via the native Далі choice. Exact lesson content and coverage remain pending; S1 inventory anchors are provisional. Practical outcome: build a short noun phrase describing a familiar person or object and indicating possession. Complete sentence production is developed in A1-03; these tasks must not silently require unintroduced copulas or conjugations.

Foundation: reuse A1-01 written forms and familiar-material shortcuts; refresh only needed prerequisites. Introduce a small reviewed set of people/object vocabulary with meanings and contextual models. Use home, belongings, and shopping as example contexts, without assuming that a particular word list or a full situation pack is already reviewed.

| Objective ID | Observable target | Provisional inventory anchor |
|---|---|---|
| A1-02-O1 | Recall a familiar noun with an appropriate article/gender pairing, including selected common exceptions rather than inferring every gender from an ending. | S1 section 1, noun gender; exact subitems to reconcile. |
| A1-02-O2 | Select definite/indefinite articles in a short context that makes the intended reference sufficiently clear. | S1 section 3, articles. |
| A1-02-O3 | Produce singular/plural noun phrases with matching article and number. | S1 section 1, noun number, and section 3. |
| A1-02-O4 | Add a familiar qualifying adjective with appropriate gender/number agreement in an introduced pattern. | S1 sections 2.1-2.3. |
| A1-02-O5 | Express simple possession using reviewed possessives and context that resolves the intended possessor. | S1 section 5; form/meaning distinctions need full mapping. |

### Accepted lesson sequence — content pending

| Lesson ID | Focus | Practice and later return |
|---|---|---|
| A1-02-L1 | A noun with its article: learn a reviewed noun/article pair and selected common gender patterns/exceptions. | Recognize, complete, then recall a short familiar expression. Separate unknown-word help from a demonstrated agreement error. |
| A1-02-L2 | Definite and indefinite articles in introduced contexts. | Choose an article, explain the context in Ukrainian when needed, and write the short phrase. Accept alternatives if the context supports them; avoid an arbitrary single-key verdict. |
| A1-02-L3 | Singular and plural, changing the noun phrase together. | Transform, correct agreement, and write a familiar singular/plural phrase. Later combine number with adjectives and possession. |
| A1-02-L4 | Describing people/objects with familiar adjectives. | Choose/produce agreement and build a short noun phrase. Broader placement differences and unintroduced vocabulary remain for later mapped lessons. |
| A1-02-L5 | Possession: my, your, and other introduced possessors. | Begin with a small set of forms, then expand to other A1 inventory forms including Spain-relevant plural-possessor forms with supporting context. Do not demand pronoun/conjugation knowledge not yet introduced. |
| A1-02-C1 | Optional mixed check and delayed independent return. | Combine article, noun, number, adjective, and possession only where introduced. Keep help available; record separate target evidence rather than a single whole-block pass/fail. |

### Preparation, evidence, and later links

- Use the accepted C-061 starter target per small objective, with reviewed variants and later prompts. Do not interpret it as a fixed item count for the entire noun/adjective system or every noun.
- Include recognition, contextual choice, noun-phrase production, and meaning-to-phrase tasks using familiar support material. Teach any supporting word before relying on it to assess grammar.
- Hints can identify noun gender, number agreement, article meaning, or the intended possessor. Attribute help/errors locally. Ambiguous article/possessor context stays unresolved or is clarified instead of marking one reference answer uniquely correct.
- Add short prepared listening where appropriate: identify which familiar phrase/description was heard, with transcript help available. Track listening separately from written agreement. Exact clip coverage, naturalness, keys, and audio review remain pending.
- Allow saving a useful reviewed expression into personal collections and appropriate later variations. Free wording/edits retain the draft/review path. Familiar material remains accessible directly without a mandatory new placement gate.
- Review noun/article and agreement targets in A1-03 sentences and A1-04 routines, then revisit them in A2-02 descriptions/comparisons. Keep dependency checks specific to the actual target.
- Full maps of adjective forms, article uses, possession forms, exceptions, lexical lists, and valid task answers are still to be authored/reconciled and cross-reviewed. This grouping does not establish complete A1 inventory coverage or release readiness.

C-064 accepts these five lessons plus optional mixed check. Next discuss A1-03 below.

## Accepted block design: A1-03 — short sentences

Status: grouping/direction accepted on 2026-10-22 under C-065 via the native Далі choice. The proposal elaborated the accepted A1-03 outline. No lesson corpus exists. Primary inventory families to reconcile under S1: subject pronouns, indicative present, simple sentence structure, and introduced question/negative patterns. Full source mapping and language review remain pending; no fresh full-inventory verification is claimed here.

Practical outcome: write and understand a few short present-tense statements, questions, and negative sentences about a familiar person, possession, or place, selecting an introduced ser/estar/hay/tener pattern in sufficient context.

Foundation: A1-01 writing and A1-02 familiar noun phrases. Reuse reviewed words and introduce the meaning of any necessary verb/preposition or time/number expression before assessing the sentence. Do not make unfamiliar vocabulary into a diagnosis of a grammar error.

| Objective ID | Observable target | Boundaries |
|---|---|---|
| A1-03-O1 | Build an introduced short sentence and connect its subject/person to the verb form. | Introduce common patterns and pronouns gradually; accept reviewed natural subject omission where appropriate. |
| A1-03-O2 | Express basic identity, origin, or an introduced description with ser. | Contextual meaning and permitted wording need review; do not replace use distinctions with an unqualified permanent/temporary slogan. |
| A1-03-O3 | Express a known referent's location or an introduced state with estar. | Limit the first adjective/state set to reviewed uses; broader contrasts return later. |
| A1-03-O4 | Say that something is present using an introduced hay frame and distinguish it from locating a specified referent. | Supply sufficient context and avoid silent tests of untaught determiner/preposition patterns. |
| A1-03-O5 | Express possession or a small set of introduced tener meanings. | Age expressions may be included only with already introduced numbers; broader number work remains A1-05. |
| A1-03-O6 | Ask a short introduced question and produce a negative sentence. | Begin with accessible written patterns; detailed question-word coverage continues in A1-05. |

### Accepted lesson sequence — content pending

| Lesson ID | Focus | Practice and later return |
|---|---|---|
| A1-03-L1 | The short sentence: subject/person, an introduced verb pattern, and useful word order. | Build a sentence from familiar elements, complete a form, and write a short statement. Provide person/pronoun reference on demand. |
| A1-03-L2 | Ser for introduced identity/origin/description contexts. | Form practice and short meaning-to-sentence tasks. Reuse article/adjective targets selectively; record the actual target of any help. |
| A1-03-L3 | Estar for location and a small reviewed state set. | Match context and sentence, complete the form, and describe a familiar location/state. Contrast with already introduced ser only when meaning is clear. |
| A1-03-L4 | Hay and estar: what is there versus where a known thing/person is. | Use a short authored situation, contextual choice, and short production; do not force a unique verdict when both readings are supported. |
| A1-03-L5 | Tener: possession and useful introduced expressions. | Write about a familiar possession; add other meanings only with supporting vocabulary already taught. Revisit A1-02 possession from a sentence perspective. |
| A1-03-L6 | Questions and negative sentences with these patterns. | Turn an introduced statement into a question/negative, understand the written question, and give a short written answer. No spoken response is required. |
| A1-03-C1 | Optional mixed check and delayed independent return. | Mix introduced patterns with adequate context. Keep verb form, contextual selection, agreement, and any listening evidence distinct. |

### Preparation, evidence, and later links

- Start each new pattern with a short Ukrainian explanation and supported example, then independent opportunities with help available. Familiar-material shortcuts remain under C-029; no new mandatory placement/unlock threshold is introduced.
- Introduce present forms in manageable steps across persons, including Spain-relevant plural usage. Complete tables, exact first-lesson form allocation, natural subject omission, and accepted variants must be specified and reviewed; A1-04 revisits these foundations while adding broader present-tense control.
- Prepare approximately 8-12 reviewed tasks per suitable small objective under C-061, with contextual choice, short writing, and distinct later prompts where appropriate. A form task that names the verb does not by itself prove independent contextual choice.
- Hints may target person/form, verb meaning/selection, agreement, or vocabulary. Preserve partial success and uncertain answers. Broad explanations do not automatically fail the entire sentence/topic.
- Use short prepared Spain listening items to recognize the meaning of introduced statements/questions, with optional replay/transcript help and separate listening evidence. Exact clip allocation, actual-audio checks, and reviewed keys remain pending.
- Save useful reviewed statements or questions into personal collections. Learner-created variants retain draft/review boundaries. Practice remains written/listening only.
- Revisit A1-02 noun/article/adjective/possession evidence in later sentences. Bring ser/estar/hay/tener targets back in A1-04 routines, A1-05 practical questions, and A2-01/A2-02 descriptions; later tense contrast must not silently replace these present-focused targets.

## Example of later detailed design: A2-03

This example is a design sketch chosen because tense forms and exceptions motivated the product; it is not the next module to implement.

Separate skill records for auxiliary selection, regular participle formation, individual irregular forms, word order, and tense choice. A prompt naming the tense checks formation; a later prompt with sufficient context and no tense label checks selection. A missing vocabulary item must not automatically become a tense error.

Possible loop: concise rule reference -> targeted form practice -> contextual gap -> short written production -> later mixed review. Use progressive hints that identify the assistance category. Independent follow-up must use a new prompt, not merely repeat a just-revealed answer. Review and validate permissible alternative wording before release.
